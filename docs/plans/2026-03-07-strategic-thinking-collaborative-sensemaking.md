# Strategic Thinking: Collaborative Sense-Making Vision

*Brainstorming session 2026-03-07 — thinking exercise, not a build plan*

## Context

Shodoboard today is a PM-level tool: outcome-driven board + AI coaching that challenges feature factory thinking. It works well at that level. This thinking explores what could exist **alongside** it — not replacing, but expanding the scope.

## Two Starting Angles

### 1. Organization-Level Alignment

The problem: team-level goals don't connect to org-level intent. Leadership has a completely different picture of what is/should be going on than the teams doing the work. Goal cascade exists in theory but breaks in practice. Teams and leadership often don't share a common language (business outcomes vs. features/projects), and learnings from delivery rarely flow back up to update strategy.

### 2. AI-Driven Work Changes Everything

If AI makes building near-instant, the bottleneck shifts from "can we build it" to "should we build it" and "did it work." The PM's value moves entirely to problem definition, validation, and measurement. Organizations can run many more parallel bets, which makes alignment *harder*, not easier. The scarce resource becomes **strategic clarity and evaluation capacity**, not engineering.

## Where These Intersect

These angles form a stack: **Dream → Strategy → Goals → Outcomes → Work.** Shodoboard lives at the bottom three layers. The higher up you go, the more broken things are — and the more leverage a tool has.

If building is cheap, the gap between dream and reality isn't resources — it's **clarity and conviction.**

## The Emerging Vision

**Help people from different levels and roles dream together, then share the vision and make it reality together.**

Not top-down (leadership decides, teams execute) or bottoms-up (teams propose, leadership approves). Something more like collaborative sense-making — where perspectives genuinely influence each other and the synthesis is better than any single viewpoint.

Key elements:
- People from different levels connected: leaders, PMs, designers, developers
- Everyone gets their own thinking and sparring time
- But they also get **impacted by each other's thoughts**
- Everyone has their own view of the product and its possibilities
- The best solutions live in the synthesis of all these perspectives and the advanced thinking that emerges when people's angles influence each other

### Working Description

A space where people across an organization think out loud about what their product should become — with AI that helps each person sharpen their own thinking, surfaces connections between people's ideas, and makes it visible where genuine alignment exists vs. where people are just using the same words for different things.

## The Hard Problem

People don't naturally think out loud at work. Every tool that tries this (wikis, shared docs, brainstorming apps) follows the same pattern: initial enthusiasm, then abandonment. The critical question isn't features — it's **"why would someone open this on a Tuesday afternoon?"**

Shodoboard's answer today: the board is there, and the AI pokes you with something worth thinking about. The nudge creates a pull.

### Four Candidate Pull Mechanisms (Unresolved)

1. **"Someone thought something interesting"** — Curiosity. A designer posted a reflection that connects to something you've been thinking about.
2. **"The AI challenged me"** — Personal provocation. The AI noticed a tension between what you said matters and what's actually happening.
3. **"I can see my impact"** — Visibility. Your technical insight actually changed how leadership framed next quarter's ambition.
4. **"It's where my thinking lives"** — Personal value. A professional journal that happens to be connected to everyone else's.

## Open Questions for Next Session

- Which pull mechanism feels strongest? Could multiple combine?
- What's the smallest version of this that would be worth trying?
- Is this an extension of Shodoboard (add layers above the PM board) or a separate product that connects to it?
- What format does "thinking out loud" actually take? Freeform text? Structured reflections? Reactions to prompts?
- How does AI facilitate without homogenizing? (Risk: AI synthesis flattens genuinely different viewpoints into corporate-safe consensus)
- Who would try this first? A team you know? A specific type of organization?
- How does the "dreaming" part work — what would make people dare to dream ambitiously at work?
